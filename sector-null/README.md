# Sector Null

A top-down sci-fi survival shooter and competition remix of [Abandon All Hope by Josh Werner](https://codepen.io/editor/joshwerner/pen/01a18d27-5cbe-77c4-9685-9f3836db15e6). Keep the reactor deck clear as rogue security units breach the bulkheads. After each wave, the operator stands still while you choose an upgrade.

## Status

Playable first build. The wave system, drone pathing, destructible entrances and character animation are adapted from the supplied original. Sector Null has a new Three.js reactor deck, armoured operator, security drones, interface and procedural Web Audio effects. The Core Zero art direction informed the smoked glass, machined hardware and cyan and amber lighting. Local high score and settings are saved in the browser. The global leaderboard is labelled Top 25 runs and shows those runs in a scrollable list and is Neon-backed (name, score, wave, kills, build, timestamp; build/time stay server-side only). On death (after finishing wave 1) the game asks for a 3-character callsign (A–Z, 0–9), prefills the last one from local storage, and posts the score. The simplified game-over screen shows the run summary, name entry and replay/menu actions with cyan and magenta frames. Keyboard/mouse/touch use a text field requiring exactly 3 characters; validation and submission errors replace the Enter 3 letters hint in the same line and clear when editing; controller mode uses arcade letter reels, then the post-run leaderboard with Play again / Main menu. Both leaderboards share the same rank / score / wave / name columns, row renderer, first-place styling and scroll treatment. A successful submission uses the returned run ID to highlight only that run and the server-provided overall rank for the placement message. Top-25 runs scroll into view; runs outside the returned top 25 remain pinned beneath a divider with their true rank. If a response lacks rank data, the screen confirms submission and shows Rank unavailable rather than guessing a position. The post-run list supports touch, wheel, keyboard focus and controller navigation (up/down scroll while the list is focused, left/right move to actions).

Scoring, emerald pickups, accuracy and high-score rules are documented in [SCORING.md](./SCORING.md).

### Defence Bay (staged, preview only)

Stage 1 keeps the original room-1 deck plate loop exactly as on main, and extends the floor past it with one cheap hole-cut plane (same dark exterior colour) covering the reserved room-2 footprint (~2.3×) plus margin. World-XZ fog (clear colour, Chebyshev from room centre, eased north) fades the outskirts so the map feels bigger; mapview disables fog. Phone/tablet landscape pulls the follow camera in to 82% offset with a clamped look-at so walls don’t over-reveal the exterior; desktop is unchanged. Intact door/window openings only block drone nav/melee when a path crosses the wall plane, and the player clamp keeps the operator out of opening slabs.

Stage 2 adds room 2’s outer shell only (walls, floor/outline, south door gap lined up with room 1’s north door, door-to-door with no corridor). Visual only — not in collision/nav; room 1 gameplay, reactor, perimeter props unchanged. Use `?dev=mapview` for a far top-down overview of both rooms. Preview builds ship to `/sector-null-preview/`; live `/sector-null/` stays on main.

## Drone roles and wave bosses

Each wave ends with one larger red boss after all regular drones are defeated. The boss has a named health bar, extra armour and reduced knockback. Outside the deck they sprint toward a door (~2.8×). Bosses use doors only, align with the centre before entering, and keep moving straight through until their whole body clears the frame before starting aimed attacks or milestone patterns. A blocked crossing resets their alignment. Cannon keeps that sprint and only sprays warning bolts through its door/window on the way in — full aimed volleys wait until it is on the deck. On death it plays a short multi-blast sequence (staggered body explosions, a final shockwave burst, light screen shake) while you stay controllable and invulnerable; drone shots are cleared and nothing new spawns. After a brief hold the upgrade picker fades in. Reduced-motion shortens or mutes the spectacle. Boss health uses a moderated per-wave curve (`6 + 1.3×wave + 0.1×wave²` times the wave's base health), with milestone attack patterns every fifth wave. Wave 1 contains grey patrols. Bosses rotate through four named tricks: Slammer (wide slam melee), Charger (rush and swipe), Cannon / Skirmisher (volley or flank), and Spawner (peels off spawns that later reform into the core). Cannon appears from wave 3 with charged volleys. All drones share the same hunt pathing; locomotion differs by role (walk, wheels, skitter, stomp). Drone shots stop at cover and are cleared between combat phases. Waves whose subtitle announces a new regular drone (1–4 and 10) open with only that type (1–2 for waves 1–4, exactly two bombardiers on wave 10); the rest of the wave holds until those debut drones die or about 4.5 seconds pass, then the normal mix continues. Total wave size is unchanged, and boss intros stay on their own banners.

Early upgrade picks bias toward firepower, and Split Beam is guaranteed among the first two wave-clear offers if you have not taken it yet.

| Drone | First wave | Health multiplier | Movement and attack |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grey Patrol | 1 | 1× | Walking pursuit and melee strike |
| Cyan Flanker | 2 | 0.7× | Wheeled, faster, smaller |
| Purple Pulse Gunner | 3 | 1.25× | Walking strafe at range, charged bolts |
| Gold Bulwark | 4 | 2.5× | Stomping heavy; wind-up strikes deal 2 shield damage |
| White Bombardier | 10 | 0.55× | Compact, fast tracked mortar tank; one marked blast over walls every 5 seconds |

Health multipliers apply to the wave's base health and round up. Boss melee and projectiles deal 1 shield damage. Projectile speed stays at 8 units/second and drone movement caps at 5.5, below the operator's base speed. Colour identifies the role; size, cannon silhouettes and movement provide additional cues. The boss intro banner shows only the name.

Wave 10 opens with exactly two **White Bombardiers** alone (same intro hold as other debut waves: until they die or ~4.5s), then the normal mix. Later waves allow up to three across the wave, with only one shell in flight at a time. White armour, a low hull, dark tracks and a raised mortar barrel distinguish it from humanoid drones and the player's floating helper. Its hull is 30% smaller than the original tank, with matching navigation clearance for doors. It moves at 1.05× patrol speed and has 0.55× patrol health (4 HP at wave 10), making it a quick priority kill. Its independent turret starts firing 1.5 seconds after spawning and keeps firing while approaching, breaking through doors, and repositioning, without range or line-of-sight restrictions. It must fully clear the doorway before taking up an artillery position. Each shell locks one amber target circle at the operator's position. Every tank's first shell gives a 3-second ground warning before impact; subsequent shells give 1.6 seconds. Mortar rings stay bright from the start, and the warning sound comes from the impact marker so off-screen tanks remain audible. A finned rocket (nose, body, exhaust trail) arcs toward the locked marker — same path and timing as before — and explodes for one shield damage within a 1.8-unit radius. It fires at most once every five seconds, including at close range, and repositions to maintain distance. There is no lingering damage patch. Mortar warnings and shells clear before the boss arrives and when combat resets; timers pause with gameplay. These are initial balance values, intended to add target priority without a barrage of overlapping zones.

From about wave 12 the game soft-caps debris, drone bolts and live score gems, trims spark counts, and budgets SFX voices so late runs (wave 25+) stay responsive without changing early-wave feel. Overflow gems still bank their score. After a hitch the loop briefly sheds cosmetic work and caps the player move step so control doesn’t feel stuck then suddenly lunge.

## Navigation and power-up feedback

Uncollected supplies expire after 6 seconds of active play. During the final 2 seconds they blink twice per second and their ground ring shrinks. Reduced-motion mode keeps them visible and uses only the shrinking ring. Drop timers pause while choosing an upgrade or pausing the game.

Shield pickups are rare: while injured, eligible kills have a separate base 0.5% chance to drop one, capped at one shield drop per wave (including drops that expire). Emergency Pulse kills cannot drop shields, including bosses killed by the pulse. Salvage Scanner and the guaranteed-supply rule apply only to Overcharge and Infinite Cell, never shields. After wave 5, all drop chances (including scanner bonuses and shields) scale by `20 / (5 + 3 × wave)` to offset the growing drone count. The guarantee stretches from 30 dry kills to `ceil(30 / scale)`, so it cannot bypass the reduction. At wave 50, the base regular-supply chance is about 0.65% per kill, the maximum scanner chance is 1.81%, and the shield chance is about 0.065%; the supply guarantee takes 233 dry kills. Scanner cards display the current scaled chance. There is no automatic shield refill on wave clear. If you are below max shields when a wave clears (or a boss dies), a large pulsing +1 SHIELD pickup spawns on the floor near the last kill — reachable inside the arena, never in walls. It stays through the upgrade screen; once the next wave starts, after about one second it begins the usual blink/fade despawn (one second shorter than other pickups) (at most one of these clear-shields at a time). Toast, pip glow and heal chime fire only on pickup. Boss kills still drop that shield when injured, or a modest bonus gem burst when shields are already full. Regular boss powerups (Overcharge / Infinite Cell) stay on the floor through the upgrade screen too — same 1s post-wave grace and shortened blink/fade as the clear shield, and they can sit alongside it. All boss gems fly straight to the operator with the normal collect sound/score (picker still opens promptly after the death sequence); any leftovers bank at clear. The shield pickup stays on the floor for a manual grab. Chosen shield upgrades still provide recovery.

Drones plan around both cover and bulkheads using navigation graphs sized to their bodies. Routes refresh as the target moves, with stable corner choices to limit jitter. Movement checks clearance before stepping, turns in place for sharp corners, and limits steps at waypoints. Ranged strafing uses the same navigation; crowd separation avoids pushing drones into obstacles. Melee attacks cannot pass through solid cover.

Power-ups light the operator orange for Overcharge and blue for Infinite Cell with a strong pulsing glow (steady under reduced motion). Peaks flash toward white so the buff feels active. If both are active, the torso and limbs pulse on separate phases. Shield kits produce a short pink glow. Repeat Overcharge pickups add 10 seconds and Infinite Cell pickups add 8 seconds to the remaining duration; damage stays at 2×. Extra stored time strengthens the glow and shows EXTENDED beside the active timer. What each power-up does is taught once on first pickup; the HUD timer does not repeat that explanation. Magnetic Gems shows as a soft green circle around the operator once owned — not a timed HUD line.

## Run locally

The leaderboard uses a themed native scroll area inspired by shadcn/ui: a slim cyan scrollbar with pink hover/active feedback, fixed column headings, and position-aware fades that disappear at each end. The same scrollbar and fade treatment applies to overflowing menu panels. Touch swipes, wheel/trackpad input and dragging the desktop scrollbar use browser-native scrolling. On the leaderboard, arrows/W/S move by a row, Page Up/Down and Space/Shift+Space move by a page, and Home/End jump to either end. Narrow layouts keep all score columns visible; high-contrast mode removes masks and reduced-motion mode disables panel resizing animation. No React or additional UI dependencies are required.

### Neon agent tooling

The official Neon skills are installed for this project in `.agents/skills/` with source hashes in `skills-lock.json`: Neon overview, Postgres, Functions, Auth, Object Storage, AI Gateway and Realtime. Existing application services and credentials are reused; installing skills does not enable those optional services.

Neon CLI 9.3.0 is installed on the setup machine. The local, git-ignored `.neon` file links this directory to the existing Sector Null project and its `main` branch. Linking uses `--no-env-pull --no-config` to preserve environment files and avoid generating infrastructure configuration. Other machines can link with:

```sh
neon link --project-id orange-sea-32353718 --branch main --no-env-pull --no-config -y
```

The setup machine's Codex user configuration has a Neon MCP entry pinned to this project, authenticated by a project-scoped API key stored outside the repository. Other Codex settings and MCP entries are preserved. This connection can modify resources within Sector Null; it cannot access other projects. Skills are project files; MCP authentication remains machine-local.

Deploy only the leaderboard function, preserving other services and existing function environment variables:

```sh
neon function deploy scores --src functions/scores/index.ts
```

GitHub Pages deployment does not deploy Neon Functions. The `scores` function returns up to 25 leaderboard entries. During setup, `neon skills status` incorrectly marked freshly installed skills as outdated while `neon skills update -y` reported all current; all 35 installed files were independently confirmed to match the upstream repository.

### Game development

Use Node.js 22 or later.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Wave-clear upgrade cards share `upgrades.js` (catalog + `stat()`), `upgrade-card.js` / `upgrade-cards.css` (chrome), and `upgrade-icons.js` (3D dioramas). Open `upgrade-cards-3d.html` via the Vite server to preview every offer rank offline.

`npm run build` writes a static site to `dist/`. Use `npm run preview` to serve that build locally. Each build stamps a sequential `BUILD` number from the sector-null git commit count (override with `SECTOR_NULL_BUILD`); it is attached to submitted scores, not shown on the title screen. The live board URL defaults to the Neon Function endpoint (override with `SECTOR_NULL_SCORES_API`). Function source lives in `functions/scores/`. Submissions go only through that Neon Function (no anonymous table inserts): the game `start_run`s a token at run start, then `submit`s with it. The server rejects unknown/reused tokens, unfinished wave-1 runs, implausibly fast or high scores, and rate-limited IPs (hashed only). Submitted scores also store hidden nullable `input` (`touch` / `pointer` / `gamepad` / `mixed`) and `device` (`phone` / `tablet` / `desktop`) columns — the board UI does not show them yet. Rejected uploads show a short status on the death screen. The browser needs WebGL. The menu track loops across the intro and main menu. If the browser blocks autoplay, use the title screen's Start prompt to enable sound. Selecting Play switches to the gameplay score.

The gameplay score uses the full-length loops in `assets/score/` (early combat, late combat, and upgrade-break). Clearing a wave crossfades over 0.9 seconds into the upgrade loop at 38% gain with a 1 kHz low-pass filter, giving the break a quieter, softer sound. When the next wave is queued, ability-select keeps playing until wave start, then crossfades over 0.9 seconds into combat (overlapping fade-out/fade-in, same window as wave-clear). Decoded buffers for all three loops stay warm so the incoming track can start on time. Waves 1–4 use the early loop and wave 5 onward uses the late loop. Wave 4 switches on the next bar with a 0.6-second crossfade if eight drones are alive at once. The late loop has a 0.65 gain trim to compensate for its louder recording; overall gameplay score gain is 3.8, down from 5.5. Pausing freezes the music and wave timing together.

Supply pickup notices appear one at a time. The first pickup of each type in every run shows a short explanation for 4.5 seconds: +1 Shield restores one shield, Overcharge doubles damage for 10 seconds, and Infinite Cell gives unlimited ammo for 8 seconds. Later pickups in the same run show only the name. Learning notices take priority over routine notices without interrupting another explanation. Their reading timer waits until the upgrade picker is fully closed. Lesson history resets each run, so an earlier session cannot suppress a new explanation. Wave title and subtitle banners use that same 4.5-second hold so the omen text is readable.

**Magnetic Gems** is a permanent green **ability** upgrade. It appears in the normal three-card wave-clear picker as an alternative to firepower / engineer / survival — take it instead of another option when you want the score path. Once picked it stays for the run, marks the operator with a soft green floor circle, and pulls score gems through walls at a readable surge. Timed supplies (Overcharge / Infinite Cell) stay short combat spikes.

The bottom-right weapon display shows your current magazine and adds visible rifle parts as you pick weapon upgrades. While recharging, the operator also plays an in-world energy-cell reload (gun dips, spent cell ejects and fades on the deck, free hand seats a fresh glowing cell) timed to the actual Hot Swap reload duration so firing resumes exactly when the animation ends.

The pulse rifle uses a low, weighty energy pulse with a short midrange attack and bass impact. The recharge hum is subdued, with a single soft ready tone. Shot pitch gradually drops by up to three semitones as the cell empties. At one remaining charge, a low-energy beep and amber LAST CHARGE label warn you; recharging shows RECHARGING… and a progress bar. A power-down tone leads into a rising charge hum timed to the recharge duration, followed by a ready chirp and cyan CELL READY label. These labels also appear on mobile. Infinite Cell keeps full-energy shot pitch and suppresses low-charge warnings and manual recharging. Pausing freezes the charge audio; interrupted recharges stop their hum.

Destroyed drones burst into sparks and scattered parts. Reduced-motion settings suppress the blast flash and cut the spark count.

| Action | Keyboard and mouse | Touch | Xbox / standard gamepad |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Move | WASD or arrow keys | Plant and drag on the left side (also turns the gun) | Left stick (radial deadzone) |
| Aim and fire | Mouse and left click | Plant the right thumb to fire; drag to aim independently | Right stick aims and fires (Geometry Wars-style); release stops fire, last aim kept |
| Recharge | R | Tap the weapon panel | X or LB |
| Pause | Esc or P | Menu icon in the score panel | Menu / Start |
| Choose an upgrade | 1–3 or click | Tap a card | D-pad / left stick + A |
| Menus | Arrows / Enter / Esc | Tap | D-pad or left stick, A confirm, B back |
| Callsign (Game Over) | Type 3 characters + Submit | Type / Submit | Arcade reels: up/down spin letter, left/right move (right past last → Submit), A confirm, B back |
| Volume | Title Settings or pause menu | Title Settings or pause menu | Title Settings or pause menu (D-pad / stick + A) |

Touch sticks are dynamic: each thumb creates a virtual stick where it lands. If a thumb drifts past the rim, the stick base follows so full input is kept. The right stick starts firing as soon as it is held, using the current facing until you drag to steer. A Bluetooth Xbox controller works in desktop browsers and in the iOS Safari / Home Screen web app via the standard Gamepad API mapping. Mixed mouse/keyboard/touch/pad input is fine mid-run; pad aim hides the mouse crosshair.

On iPhone, Add to Home Screen keeps the game full-screen (`black-translucent` status bar, `viewport-fit=cover`). HUD, gun panel, boss bar and overlays sit `18px` past `env(safe-area-inset-*)` so they clear the Dynamic Island and home indicator. On phone (touch portrait ≤500px wide, or landscape with a short viewport) the gun panel is ~72% size and hugs safe-area + ~8px; phone landscape also pulls the header flush so the right-thumb fire zone stays open. Desktop keeps the roomier layout. Safari already lays the page out below its chrome, so those insets are 0 and the layout does not change. If an older home-screen icon still sits under the status bar, remove it and add the page to the home screen again.

The canvas size follows `visualViewport` (not a one-shot `innerHeight`), and the document is `position: fixed` with overflow hidden so iOS standalone cannot scroll the page after the safe-area/visual viewport settles. Layout, renderer size and camera aspect remeasure on `resize`, `visualViewport` resize, `orientationchange` (with a delayed pass), `pageshow`, and at startup. The title menu fades in and accepts taps only after that size has been stable for a couple of frames (or ~1s). On touch devices, menu/pause/death button highlights use `:focus-visible` and clear sticky selection when How / Scores / Settings close, so a tap cannot leave SETTINGS (or similar) looking active.

## Engineer and defence paths

Upgrade cards use responsive cut-corner SVG frames, smoked-glass panels, glowing visual previews and inset stat readouts to match the reactor menus. Icon artwork uses category-specific silhouettes: bot rings are concentric with their chassis, while Magnetic Gems uses faceted crystals and inward trails above a collection ring. Other icons have no enclosing circle. The recommended option has a brighter double frame and explicit badge; pip ranks show owned and next levels. Pause and game-over screens (including volume rows, callsign reels and submit controls) share one active highlight across mouse, keyboard and controller input. Menu hover/focus/select blips use a separate UI audio bus so they still play while combat SFX and music stay suspended on pause. Arrow keys and Tab navigate pause controls without leaving stale highlights. The main menu retains its existing selection behaviour. The picker follows the active input: controller or keyboard navigation suppresses a parked mouse hover, and moving the mouse clears the previous focus highlight. A connected but unused controller does not take over selection. Hover, keyboard focus and gamepad selection lift and enlarge the card with a stronger category glow and bright inner angular frame. Pressing gives a small compression before the chosen card expands; compact screens use smaller movements, and reduced-motion settings keep the frame highlight without scaling or deal animation. Owned gear is visible on the cards themselves (filled pips / UPGRADE HELPER BOT), so the picker does not repeat a build summary underneath. Recommendations favour an urgently needed shield, a first Split Beam, or continuing owned equipment. All three cards remain deliberate choices; nothing is automatically selected. Use 1–3, tap, or Tab and Enter. Offers include an engineer option whenever an eligible one remains, and owned modules receive higher offer weight. At most one Helper Bot-family card (Helper Bot / Upgrade Bot, Twin Guns, Bomb Launcher) appears per offer; other slots fill from the rest of the pool, and extra bot cards only return when nothing else can fill a slot. Magnetic Gems appears only in slot three when offered and is never recommended. Its third-slot position and keyboard shortcut stay fixed even when fewer upgrades remain. Pick it instead of another upgrade when you want the score path.

- **Helper Bot (3 ranks):** a lavender four-plate armour shell over a dark core with a cyan muzzle; orbits the operator at shoulder height and fires automatically. It keeps a minimum clearance and moves on a polar path around the operator (never through the model), slips out of walls when it can, turns quickly onto its target, and only fires once the barrels are roughly aligned — shots leave along the barrel tips. With no target it faces the operator’s aim/move direction. Ranks increase shot damage only. Specialise in **Twin Guns (2 ranks)** for doubled cyan barrels then faster firing, or **Bomb Launcher (2 ranks)** for a wide violet-bore launcher then a wider blast. Card icons and the in-game mesh share one builder so variants stay matched. These specialisations are mutually exclusive for that run. Bot fire does not affect rifle accuracy bonuses.
- **Trail Mines (3 ranks):** automatically deploy every 8 seconds at every rank, with at most three active. Ranks increase damage (4/5/6) and blast radius (2/2.2/2.4 units), not deployment frequency. Mines last 18 seconds and arm after 0.6 seconds. **Shock Mines** adds a 60% movement slow for three seconds to blast survivors. This keeps mines as occasional positioning tools rather than a constant source of area damage.
- **Shield Regen (2 ranks):** restores one shield after 12/8 seconds without damage, repeating while injured. **Emergency Pulse (3 ranks):** taking damage triggers an area blast and brief slow; ranks increase range and damage.

Equipment uses active combat time and pauses with the game or upgrade selection. Mines, warning zones and effects clear between waves; all equipment resets for a new run. Automated equipment deliberately adds no touch buttons.

## Milestone encounters

Waves 5, 10, 15 and onward in multiples of five replace the regular boss with the Reactor Warden / Null Overseer. Reactor lighting gradually shifts to amber or violet during the encounter. The boss marks three ground zones (four from wave 10) with a 1.4-second warning, then each ring detonates as a reactor blast (shockwave, sparks, lightning column, 1 shield if you are still inside). Its exposed phase grants 75% bonus incoming damage for 3.2 seconds, followed by pursuit before the next cycle. Move out of the marked rings and use that opening to attack.

Boss base health scaling is now `6 + 1.3×wave + 0.1×wave²` times regular wave health; milestone bosses use 1.2× this value. This reduces health-only attrition while new attack patterns provide pressure. Remaining emeralds are banked automatically at victory, including the final boss drop, before the upgrade screen opens.

Helper Bot repeat offers explicitly say UPGRADE BOT, show the damage change only, and confirm the upgrade after selection. Fire-speed gains come from Twin Guns rank 2, not from Helper Bot ranks.

Movement input is cleared when entering/leaving upgrades, starting a run, pausing or losing window/tab focus. Gameplay focus returns to the canvas after UI choices. Arrow keys suppress browser scrolling during a run; key releases are captured at window level, and switching between keyboard movement and touch clears the previous movement source. These safeguards prevent stale inputs from carrying across UI transitions.

For opposite keyboard directions, the newest press wins on each axis (including mixed WASD/arrow bindings); releasing it restores any still-held opposite direction. OS key-repeat does not change priority. This allows immediate reversals without opposite inputs cancelling movement.
