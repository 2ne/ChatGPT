# Question content review, 29 October 2026

Scope: all 570 questions in 57 shipped activities across 19 curriculum nodes.
This is a source-level content and representation review, not classroom or device testing.

## Reference
- England EYFS Number and Numerical Patterns early learning goals.
- England mathematics programmes of study, Year 1.
- Local curriculum/maths.md, QUALITY.md and AGENTS.md.

## Corrections
- Pairing questions identify a block without a pair. The Hard example uses 3, so a complete pair and an unpaired block are visible.
- Equal sharing explicitly says equally and uses groups consistently.
- Ten-frame prompts name blocks, not the fruit retained in legacy item metadata.
- Dice comparisons ask about dots.
- Single-choice comparisons use most/fewest or smallest to identify one answer among several groups.
- Two comparison questions previously had two valid answers below their threshold. They now ask for the fewest.
- Wrong-number sequences no longer repeat the same numeral in both a valid and an invalid position. Narration follows the revised sequence.
- The hundred-square left-of-21 item no longer implies wrapping across a row.
- Ordering explanations no longer claim non-consecutive numbers increase by one.
- The Year 1 two-step duck story is now one-step, with a matching join picture.
- Shape naming avoids offering Square and Rectangle, or Cube and Cuboid, as competing names when both apply.
- Correct pictured choices appear as pictures in feedback. Accessible descriptions explicitly describe complete pairs and an unpaired block.
- Removed technical family wording and several instructions from explanations.
- Unpictured Sam stories use a concrete noun instead of an unresolved object placeholder.
- Hyphenated number answers also accept spaces.

122 questions changed. Question ids and progress storage are retained.

## Prevention
Content validation checks binary answers, explicit equal sharing, unique wrong-number occurrences and consecutive-number claims. Standard missing-part wording is deliberately exempt from stem repetition limits: varying the wording merely to pass a variety rule made the language less consistent. Duplicate identical questions are still rejected.
