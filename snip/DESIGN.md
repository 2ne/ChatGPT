# Snip design system

## Direction

Snip should feel like a well-run independent barbershop: direct, precise and recognisable. The interface uses the visual language of appointment books, price boards and traditional barber signage without becoming nostalgic or decorative.

## Principles

- Start with the customer’s job and keep the next action obvious.
- Prefer borders, spacing and type hierarchy over containers and shadows.
- Use one subject-specific signature: the narrow red, white and blue barber stripe.
- Keep corners restrained and avoid nested rounded cards.
- Use colour to communicate selection and action, not to decorate.
- Use real barbershop language and specific action labels.

## Tokens

- Ink: `#111111`
- Paper: `#ffffff`
- Subtle surface: `#f7f7f5`
- Rule: `#dededb`
- Secondary text: `#666662`
- Barber red: `#d92d20`
- Barber blue: `#174ea6`

Display type uses Barlow Condensed for the economy and confidence of shop signage. Body and controls use Geist for clarity. Dates and time slots use tabular numerals.

## Components

- Primary actions are black rectangular buttons with a clear verb.
- Selections use a strong black border and a red edge, not a tinted card.
- Booking stages are a compact progress rail, not decorative numbered badges.
- Customer and admin views share the same typography, rules and spacing.
- Focus states must remain visible and work independently of colour.

## Avoid

- Cream backgrounds, green SaaS palettes and soft lifestyle gradients
- Decorative eyebrow text that does not encode useful context
- Excessive pills, shadows, floating cards and large corner radii
- Generic labels such as “Continue” when the next action is known
- Animation without a functional purpose
